Processes that once worked well become increasingly manual. Spreadsheets multiply. Systems do not quite talk to each other. Reporting takes too long. Valuable finance people spend too much time producing information and not enough time using it.

Does your finance function still fit your business?

A finance function rarely becomes inefficient overnight. More often, a business grows around it.

New people join. New systems are added. Spreadsheets fill the gaps. Processes are adapted to deal with the next problem. Workarounds gradually become permanent.

Eventually, the finance team can find itself working incredibly hard just to keep everything moving.

You might recognise some of these signs:

Month end takes too long.
Reporting arrives too late to influence decisions.
Information has to be manually pulled together from several systems.
The same data is entered more than once.
Spreadsheets have become critical to key finance processes.
Profitability is difficult to understand by customer, product, service or project.

The warning signs often appear across people, process, systems and information at the same time.

People spend significant time checking, reconciling and correcting data.
Important knowledge sits with one or two members of the team.
Existing systems are not being used to their full potential.
You want to use automation or AI but do not know where it would genuinely add value.
The finance team has little time to improve anything because they are too busy doing the day job.
You are considering a new finance or ERP system and want to get the underlying processes right first.
These are not necessarily technology problems. They are usually finance function problems that need to be understood before the right solution can be designed.
